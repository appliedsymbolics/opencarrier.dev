Carrier API · feasibility · labels · quotes · tracking

One API for every carrier. Every choice explained.

OpenCarrier checks whether a shipment can go at all, buys labels, gets quotes and tracks packages across carriers through one REST API, on your own carrier accounts. When it picks the carrier for you, it records every option it weighed and why it chose one.

Feasibility at intake

Know an order can ship before anyone picks it.

Ask POST /v1/feasibility when an order arrives. OpenCarrier checks every enabled carrier account and service against what each one declares it can carry, and answers whether any of them can take the shipment.

  • No carrier call. The answer comes from declared capabilities alone. Nothing is priced, stored or sent to a carrier, so it is cheap enough to run on every order.
  • Packages are optional. The order is not packed yet, so send only the addresses and requirements. Package rules apply only when you send packages.
  • Every reason, by name. Each account and service comes back with stable exclusion reasons, such as country_unsupported, phone_required or signature_unsupported.
  • Accounts can say no. Refuse item classes such as hazmat or alcohol, and limit an account to regions or exclude postal prefixes. The reasons read class_refused, region_unsupported and postal_excluded.
  • Handling and payers count too. A carrier that cannot request a required handling code, or bill the named payer, comes back as handling_unsupported or payer_unsupported.
  • Deliver-by aware. A service whose declared transit days arrive after the deliver-by date is listed as slower_than_deliver_by, so you see what was too slow and not only what fits.

In Ambient OS, the warehouse system holds an order that no carrier can take at intake, with its most common reasons, for a person to decide. A purchase still quotes and plans with the carrier as usual.

Carrier choice

A decision you can audit, not a black box.

Ask for an auto plan and OpenCarrier quotes every eligible account and service at once. It drops options that break a rule, such as a deliver-by date or a cost cap, and scores the rest by price plus the expected cost of a late delivery.

  • Every candidate is kept. Quotes, delivery estimates, on-time rates and the reason each option lost are stored with the plan.
  • Policies are versioned. Each plan names the policy that made it, so a decision can be replayed and compared later.
  • You set the rules. Deliver-by date, service level from deferred to overnight, handling, signature, a maximum cost and preferred carriers.
  • You choose how it ranks. Best value, cheapest or fastest, per plan.
  • Measured beats declared. Once 20 promised deliveries in 90 days back a carrier service, best value uses your own on-time rate, and each candidate says which rate it used.

Purchase guarantee

A timeout never buys the same label twice.

Carrier APIs time out. A naive retry pays for a second label. OpenCarrier records the purchase before it calls the carrier, and a request with the same Idempotency-Key returns the stored result instead of calling again.

When a carrier's answer is lost, the purchase is marked unknown, never retried blindly, and settled by lookup or by an operator.

purchasing

Recorded with a lease before the carrier is called. A repeat request reads this row.

purchased

Tracking numbers and labels stored, in PDF and ZPL for thermal printers.

rejected

The carrier refused with a code, such as a bad address. Nothing was bought; plan again safely.

unknown

The carrier may have created it. Never retried automatically; reconciled instead.

voided

Canceled with the carrier. Voiding twice is harmless.

Your accounts

Bring your own carrier accounts and your own rates.

Connect the UPS and FedEx accounts you already have. You keep your negotiated rates and your relationship with the carrier; OpenCarrier does not resell postage.

  • Encrypted at rest. Each account's credentials are sealed with AES-256-GCM under their own data key, and are never returned by the API or written to logs.
  • Checked before use. Credentials are validated when you connect an account, and a verify call tests them with the carrier.
  • Isolated by tenant. Every read of a credential requires the tenant it belongs to.
  • One tenant per client, if you need it. A warehouse or 3PL can give a client that brings its own carrier accounts a tenant and key of its own. Ambient OS uses the client's connection first, then the warehouse's.
After the label

Know where it is, and what it really cost.

Quotes are promises. OpenCarrier keeps following each package until it is delivered, then compares what you were quoted with what the carrier billed.

One tracking feed

Every carrier's scans in one set of statuses, from label created to delivered or returned. Read them as one ordered feed, so nothing is missed between polls.

GET /v1/tracking-events?after=…

Signed webhooks

Register an endpoint and receive each event as it happens. Deliveries are signed, retried, and safe to receive twice.

OpenCarrier-Signature: v1=…

Settled cost

Import the carrier's invoice CSV. Amounts are matched to labels to the cent, and re-importing a file changes nothing.

POST /v1/invoices

Carrier outcomes report

For any period: delivered packages, on-time rate, average days late, and quoted against settled cost, for each carrier and service. The numbers the next carrier choice should be based on.

GET /v1/reports/outcomes?from=…&to=…
API

Four calls from intent to label.

Describe what has to move. Get a plan. Buy it. Print it. Every call is documented in OpenAPI and authenticated with a per-tenant key.

# 4. Print it on a thermal printer
curl $OC/v1/shipments/$ID/labels/0?format=zpl

Roadmap · planned, not shipped

What carriers will be able to tell you next.

Feasibility and carrier choice decide from what each carrier account declares. The next carrier work closes the gaps below. None of this is available yet.

  • Planned

    Handling on UPS and FedEx

    Saturday delivery, liftgate and temperature control requested through each carrier. Until then, a shipment that needs them is not eligible there.

  • Planned

    Recipient and third-party billing

    UPS and FedEx labels billed to an account other than the shipper's. Until then, those shipments are not eligible there.

  • Planned

    Price basis

    Rank options by what you bill, or by margin, as well as by carrier cost.

  • Planned

    Freight

    Shipments beyond parcel, through the same API.
